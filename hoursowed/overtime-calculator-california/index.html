The rules this calculator uses

For most non-exempt employees 18 and older (and some 16- and 17-year-olds), California requires one and one-half times the regular rate for hours over 8 in a workday and over 40 in a workweek, and double the regular rate for hours over 12 in a workday (California Department of Industrial Relations, Overtime FAQ). Federal law also requires time and a half after 40 hours in a workweek (U.S. Department of Labor).

Hours already paid as daily overtime are not counted again toward the weekly 40, so nothing is paid twice.

What it does not calculate

  • The seventh consecutive workday in a workweek, which has its own overtime and double time rules in California.
  • Alternative workweek schedules (for example, four 10-hour days) and the exceptions some industries have.
  • Split shift premiums and meal or rest period premiums.

If one of these applies to you, the result is a starting point, not your final pay. Check the exceptions to the general overtime law or ask your employer.

Questions

I work in another state. Can I use this?

Turn off Daily overtime to count only weekly overtime after 40 hours, which is the federal rule. A few other states have daily overtime too; check your state labor office.

Is this legal advice?

No. It is an estimate based on the general rules above, not legal or payroll advice.
