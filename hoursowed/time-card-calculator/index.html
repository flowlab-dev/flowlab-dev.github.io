How to use it

  1. Put in your hourly rate if you want to see pay. Hours work without it.
  2. For each day, enter when you started and finished. Worked 8–10 am and then 12–4:45 pm? Press Add split shift and enter both parts.
  3. Enter unpaid breaks in minutes, for example a 30-minute lunch.
  4. A shift that ends after midnight, like 10 pm – 6 am, is counted as 8 hours on the day it started.

How overtime is worked out

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees get at least time and a half for hours over 40 in a workweek (U.S. Department of Labor). That is the default here: 40 hours, 1.5×.

Some states also count overtime by the day. In California it is 1.5× for hours over 8 in a workday and double time over 12 (California DIR). Turn on Daily overtime to count hours over 8 a day at 1.5× and hours over 12 a day at 2×. Hours already paid as daily overtime are not counted again toward the weekly 40. California’s seventh-consecutive-day rules are not calculated here.

Questions

Is this calculator free?

Yes, with no account and no limits. It saves your week in this browser so you can come back to it.

Does it round my time?

No. It counts exact minutes. If your employer rounds to the nearest 5 or 15 minutes, your paycheck can differ by a few minutes.

Is gross pay what I will get?

No. Gross pay is before taxes and deductions. It also assumes one hourly rate; bonuses and shift differentials change the overtime rate.

Can I use it for payroll?

It is an estimate to check your own hours, not legal or payroll advice. If the numbers do not match your paycheck, ask your employer or your state labor office.
